01 — Mechatronics

Part sorting automated system

Final project for MECH 458 (Mechatronics) at UVic, built with Benjamin Kappers. A conveyor carries parts past optical and reflective sensors that classify each one as aluminum, steel, white plastic, or black plastic, then a stepper-driven carousel rotates the matching bin into place before the part drops.

  • Sorted 48 parts in 39 seconds at 98% accuracy in the final demonstration.
  • Interrupt-driven state machine in C on an ATmega2560, with a FIFO queue tracking part order between sensing and sorting.
  • Materials classified by the lowest ADC reflectance reading against bounds calibrated from repeated test runs.
  • Precomputed quintic S-curve stepper profile (Python) to cut mechanical shock and prevent missed steps.
  • Tuned belt duty cycle and stepper timing to trade top speed for reliable classification and bin alignment.

04 — Design for manufacture

Ergonomic shift knob — CAD and 3-axis CNC milling

An ergonomic replacement shift knob designed for machining rather than printing: a two-piece 303 stainless assembly with push-on retention that can be removed for service.

  • Split into two pieces so each half can be machined in a single setup, avoiding fourth-axis work.
  • Push-on retention using a machined groove and spring clip rather than set screws or adhesive.
  • Fit governed by the clip groove tolerance rather than the bore.
  • Toolpaths programmed in Mastercam and proven out in Vericut before cutting metal.

Process
